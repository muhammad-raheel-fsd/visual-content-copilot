# Style research: reference channels (2026-10-10)

Condensed from a web research pass. Items marked **[observed]** are common knowledge about the
channel that no source confirmed: watch 3 to 5 videos before relying on them.

## Fireship (verified)

- Series: "X in 100 Seconds", The Code Report, tutorials. Faceless; memes and movie clips fill
  the screen. ([Engineer's Codex](https://read.engineerscodex.com/p/how-fireship-became-youtubes-favorite))
- Workflow, in Jeff Delaney's own words ([transcript](https://gotranscript.com/public/how-i-built-a-successful-youtube-channel-with-programming-tutorials)):
  - Voiceover first, almost sentence by sentence; visuals are added after.
  - A graphic "typically only stays there for 1 to 5 seconds"; keep pauses to a minimum.
  - Code appears to type itself (he records an undo of deleted code); frame holds for reading.
  - "The first 60 seconds is your best opportunity to set the tone."
  - Tools: Premiere, After Effects, Figma, FlatIcon icons, stock and meme sites. A USB mic
    under $100, plus loudness matching and a de-esser.
- Hook pattern: "[Tech], a [one-line definition]", then straight into a command or example.
  Git in 100 Seconds opens that way. ([transcript](https://sozai.app/transcript/git-explained-100-seconds/))
- Speaking pace about 150 words a minute with no dead air (estimated from one caption file).
- Ending: one sentence ("hit the like button, subscribe..."), no long outro.
- [observed] Dark background, tech logos, quick zoom punches, text pop-ups, memes as punchlines.

## Slaying The Dragon (the "Slay the Dragon" you remembered)

- Real channel (@slayingthedragon): web development (CSS, JavaScript), about 149K subscribers,
  16 videos, now inactive. ([developereducators.com](https://developereducators.com/channel/slayingthedragon/))
- Known for "Learn Flexbox CSS in 8 minutes" and "Learn CSS Grid - A 13 Minute Deep Dive":
  each property's visual effect is shown live, faster than reading the docs.
- Takeaways: the "Learn X in N minutes" title promise; show the effect, not just the syntax.

## No Fluff Academy

- Real channel (@NoFluffAcademy), but it covers maths, physics, chemistry and biology, not CS.
  Titles: "[Topic] Explained Simply", "Explained Like You Are 5 Years Old".
  ([site](https://nofluffacademy.netlify.app/))
- Teaching rules worth copying: idea before formula, real-world examples first, write "for
  someone smart who was taught badly", zero jargon.

## ByteByteGo (added: the closest match for diagram explainers)

- Alex Xu and Sahn Lam (System Design Interview books), about 1.4M subscribers. Short,
  diagram-driven, faceless. ([blog](https://blog.bytebytego.com/p/new-system-design-youtube-channel))
- Visual grammar (described by imitators): real tech icons instead of boxes, dots or pulses
  travelling along connectors, parts revealed in order, minimal labels, left-to-right flow,
  never the full diagram at once. ([dev.to](https://dev.to/rahishsaifi/how-to-create-bytebytego-like-animated-diagrams-for-free-4ece))
- Numbered steps (1, 2, 3, 4) and an "Over to you" question at the end.
  ([HTTPS episode](https://blog.bytebytego.com/p/how-https-works-youtube-diagram-as))
- [observed] Calm narration, pastel rounded boxes, little humour, light music.

## easy, actually (Muhammad's pick, 2026-10-10)

- Channel `@easyactually`, English. Tagline: "explaining stuff with few word and more picture".
  Life and study topics, plus "coding is easy, actually" and "building an app is easy, actually".
  Recent videos get roughly 70K to 1.8M views (channel feed, 2026-10-10).
- **The brand name IS the title formula:** every title is "<topic> is easy, actually". One
  phrase is the name, the promise and the series, so every title repeats the brand.
- **Thumbnails, one template** (seen on two of them):
  - pure black background with a thin yellow frame;
  - a small lowercase lead-in in white ("how to", "a guide to");
  - one big yellow keyword with a hand-drawn squiggle underline;
  - stick figures, real logos (C, Python, JS, the App Store) and one funny cut-out or game sprite;
  - one small diagram (a Venn diagram).
- **Lessons for us:**
  - a fixed title formula that carries the brand;
  - one thumbnail template so every video is recognisable in a feed;
  - lowercase, friendly words;
  - the picture does the explaining and the words are few.

## Shorts and Reels: retention facts

- TikTok: put the content's promise in the first 3 seconds; do not open with a logo.
  ([TikTok creative best practices](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices))
- YouTube counts every start or replay of a Short as a view since 31 March 2025; judge with
  "engaged views". ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted))
- Instagram's top signals (January 2025): watch time, likes per reach, sends per reach; sends
  matter most for reaching non-followers. ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-2025/738034/))
- Captions: many people watch muted in public, and most are more likely to finish a captioned
  video. ([3Play Media](https://www.3playmedia.com/blog/verizon-media-and-publicis-media-find-viewers-want-captions/))
  For second-language learners keep captions short and next to the visual (redundancy effect,
  Clark and Mayer).
- Shorter videos are more engaging; the speaker's pace and enthusiasm, and drawing over the
  visuals, raise engagement. ([Guo, Kim, Rubin 2014](https://up.csail.mit.edu/other-pubs/las2014-pguo-engagement.pdf))
- Keep key text in the centre, away from the right-hand button rail and the bottom caption
  area; check each platform's preview.
